Summary

Rivers is a proof-of-stake Layer 1 blockchain. It is a fork of Sui, so it keeps Sui's Move smart contract language, its object data model and its Mysticeti consensus. Rivers changes the native token, the supply, the reward schedule and the validator rules, and adds its own applications: a self-custody wallet, an explorer, a token launcher, an exchange, a lending market, investor vesting and a payment network for banks.

The native token is RVRB. At most 21,000,000 RVRB will ever exist. Ten million exist at launch, and eleven million are paid to stakers over fifty years.

Rivers is designed and built in Nigeria. The products show prices in naira, and the payment network is shaped around how Nigerian banks and payment firms settle with each other.

Why Rivers

Money moves between Nigerian institutions every day through several separate systems. Each institution keeps its own ledger, and the ledgers are reconciled after the fact. A shared ledger that every member can read, with settlement in one step, removes much of that reconciliation.

People also want to hold value in more than one currency, to save, to earn from staking and to borrow against what they own, from a phone. Rivers puts these in one wallet where the person holds their own keys.

Builders need a chain where fees are low, contracts are safe by default and tools are mature. Rivers inherits Move and the Sui tooling, so existing Sui knowledge carries over.

Architecture

Objects and Move

Every asset on Rivers is an object with an owner and a version. Coins, liquidity shares, vesting schedules and administrator rights are all objects. Move's type system means a coin cannot be copied or dropped by mistake, and a function that needs an administrator right must be handed the object that grants it.

Consensus

A committee of validators orders transactions with Mysticeti, a consensus protocol that builds a graph of blocks proposed by all validators at once. Transactions that touch unrelated objects execute in parallel. A transaction is final when the committee has committed it; there are no forks to wait out.

Epochs

The network runs in epochs of 24 hours. At the end of each epoch the committee changes to reflect new stake, rewards are paid and protocol changes take effect.

Units

One RVRB is 1,000,000,000 of the smallest unit. Rivers apps call the smallest unit a drop. Fees are paid in RVRB.

Access

Nodes serve a gRPC interface, which the Rivers apps reach with gRPC-Web from the browser. A Rivers indexer reads every checkpoint into a database and serves summaries, charts, holders, tokens and pool data over a simple HTTP API.

Validators and staking

Anyone with a server and enough stake can become a validator; no approval is needed. RVRB holders who do not run servers delegate their stake to a validator and share its rewards.

Voting power follows stake, with a cap so that no single validator controls the committee. Each validator sets a commission, which it keeps from its delegators' rewards.

Vesting

Tokens for investors and team members can be locked in an on-chain vesting schedule with an unlock at the start, a cliff and linear release. The holder claims unlocked tokens from the wallet. A schedule marked revocable can be stopped by its issuer; tokens already unlocked stay with the holder.

Rivers Connect

Rivers Connect is a payment network for regulated institutions, written as a Move package. The Rivers foundation holds an operator right that admits members. Each member record holds the institution's name, city, country, tier, a payment limit, an active flag and counts of payments sent and received.

A member pays another member by calling the network's pay function with a coin, the receiving institution, a reference of up to 35 characters and a four-character purpose code. The chain checks that both institutions are members and active, that the amount is within the sender's limit and that the network is not paused. It then moves the coin and records a payment event with a running number.

Connect turns these events into ISO 20022 pacs.008 messages, so a member's existing systems can read Rivers payments in the format they already use.

On the testnet, institutions pay each other in NGNs and USDs test stablecoins. A naira or dollar stablecoin on mainnet needs a licensed issuer that holds reserves.

Exchange and lending

Rivers DEX

The exchange uses constant-product pools. Each swap pays a 0.30% fee that stays in the pool for its liquidity providers, who hold LP coins for their share. Each pool keeps running price totals over time, so other contracts can read a time-weighted average price that a single large trade cannot move.

Rivers Lend

Each lending market pairs one collateral coin with one loan coin, so a problem in one market cannot spread to another. Suppliers deposit the loan coin and receive share coins that grow in value as borrowers pay interest. Borrowers lock collateral and borrow up to a set share of its value.

Because prices come from averages, a borrower cannot push a pool price up and borrow against it in the same moment. The tests in the repository show this case being refused.

Shielded payments On testnet

Shielded payments let a person move value without showing the amount, the sender or the receiver on the public ledger. Coins enter a shielded pool, move inside it as private notes and can leave it to any address.

  • Each note is a Poseidon hash commitment to an amount, an owner key and a random value. Commitments are stored in a Merkle tree of depth 20.
  • A transaction spends up to two notes and creates two new ones. A Groth16 proof on the BN254 curve shows that the spent notes exist in the tree, belong to the sender and balance with the new notes, without revealing which notes they are.
  • Each spent note publishes a nullifier, so a note cannot be spent twice.
  • New notes are encrypted to the receiver's key, so only the receiver can find and spend them.
  • The chain verifies proofs with Sui's native Groth16 verifier.

On the testnet, the wallet's Private page shields RVRB and USDs, sends them privately to a private address and unshields them. Each proof is made on the user's device. The fee for a private send is paid from the user's public address, so observers can see that the address used the pool, but not the amount or the receiver.

The proving keys come from a trusted setup. The testnet setup has one contributor. Mainnet needs a public ceremony with many independent contributors, so that no single party can forge proofs.

Bridge Planned

The bridge will connect Rivers to Ethereum. The Sui codebase that Rivers is built from contains a native bridge design: the validator committee watches both chains and signs transfers, and contracts on each side release tokens only with enough committee signatures. Rivers will build on that design so that the bridge is secured by Rivers' own validators instead of a separate company.

Security

  • Self-custody. Recovery words never leave the user's device. The wallet encrypts them with a key derived from the password (PBKDF2 with 250,000 rounds) and AES-GCM.
  • Rights as objects. Every administrative action requires a specific capability object. The operator right for Connect, the lending admin right, the vesting issuer right and the package upgrade rights are separate objects held by the administrator address.
  • Public record. Administrative actions are on-chain transactions, so anyone can see who changed what and when.
  • Bounded settings. Lending parameters are checked against fixed limits on chain, so even the administrator cannot set an unsafe value.
  • Separate sites. Each product is its own site with a strict content security policy, so a fault in one cannot reach another's keys.
  • Audits. The Rivers packages go through internal review and automated tests; the review is published in the repository as rivers-packages/AUDIT.md. An external audit is planned before mainnet.

Administration

On the testnet, the administrator rights sit with the first foundation wallet. Rivers Core is the interface for using them. Protocol upgrades follow the Sui process: validators run the new software, and the new protocol version takes effect at an epoch boundary once enough stake supports it.

Moving the administrator rights to a multi-signature wallet held by several people is recommended before mainnet.

Roadmap

Running on the testnet

  • The Rivers chain with RVRB, the fixed supply and the reward schedule
  • Shielded payments for RVRB and USDs
  • Rivers Wallet, Scan, Connect, Validator and Core as separate sites
  • Indexer, Token Studio, investor vesting
  • Rivers DEX with time-weighted prices, and Rivers Lend
  • USDs and NGNs test stablecoins

In development

  • Paying private-send fees from an unrelated address
  • Bridge to Ethereum
  • Testnet on public servers, so outside validators can join

Before mainnet

  • External security audit
  • Public trusted setup ceremony for shielded payments
  • Genesis wallets created by their owners
  • Licensed stablecoin issuers for naira and dollars

Risks and notices

Rivers is on testnet. Test RVRB, USDs and NGNs have no value, and the testnet may be reset. Software can contain errors even after testing and audits. Prices of digital assets can fall sharply. Nothing on this site is an offer to sell tokens or financial advice. Laws on digital assets in Nigeria and elsewhere can change, and users are responsible for following the laws that apply to them.
